On a Mac the overlay runs as a menu bar app. Besides the floating session list it adds a ✻ item to the menu bar, widgets for Notification Center and the desktop, and on macOS 26 a Control Center control. It needs macOS 11 or later; the widgets need macOS 14 or later.

Pick the right DMG

There are two builds. Macs with an M1 chip or newer need the Apple Silicon DMG; older Intel Macs need the Intel DMG. If you are not sure, open the Apple menu → About This Mac: it lists either a "Chip" (Apple M-something) or a "Processor" (Intel).

  1. Open the DMG and drag Claude Session Overlay into Applications.
  2. Launch it from Applications. A ✻ icon appears in the menu bar.
  3. On first launch it asks whether to open at login. You can change this later with Open at login in its menu.

Getting past the first-launch warning

The app is not notarized by Apple yet, so the first time you open it macOS says it cannot verify the developer. This is the standard Gatekeeper check for apps from outside the App Store. To open it anyway, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway next to the message about Claude Session Overlay. macOS remembers the choice.

Alternatively, install from Terminal in one line. The script downloads the right DMG for your chip, copies the app into Applications without the quarantine flag, and launches it, so there is no warning at all:

curl -fsSL https://claude-overlay.jsuk.cloud/install.sh | bash

The menu bar item

Next to the ✻ icon the menu bar shows colored counts: yellow for sessions that need input, orange for working, green for done. Once the 5-hour usage limit passes 75% its percentage appears there too. Click it for the full session list (pick one to jump to it), weekly and 5-hour usage, and the overlay's commands: show or hide, alerts, open at login, and quit.

Widgets

Right-click the desktop → Edit Widgets and search for "Claude Session Overlay", or add them to Notification Center (click the date and time at the right of the menu bar). There are four sizes:

  • Small: counts by state and the 5-hour limit.
  • Medium: three sessions.
  • Large: seven sessions with usage bars.
  • Extra large (desktop only): counts, usage and seven sessions with their folders.

Tap a session in a widget to go to it. Elapsed time for working sessions keeps counting on its own. The widgets read a status file the overlay writes (~/.claude/session-hud/status.json); if the overlay is not running, after a few minutes they say so instead of showing old data.

Control Center (macOS 26 and later)

Open Control Center → Edit Controls and add the "Claude 세션" control to Control Center or the menu bar. It shows how many sessions need input; click it to open that session, or the overlay if none is waiting.

How jumping works on a Mac

Sessions in the Claude desktop app open through a claude:// link. Terminal sessions in Terminal or iTerm2 jump to their exact tab; macOS asks once for permission to control those apps. Sessions in other apps such as VS Code bring the app to the front. The floating overlay is visible on every Space but stays out of fullscreen apps.
